Frequently asked questions

Where should I keep my savings while working toward a goal?

For goals 1–5 years away, a high-yield savings account (HYSA) is ideal — FDIC insured, liquid, and currently earning 4–5% APY. For goals 5+ years away, consider a mix of HYSA and low-risk index funds. Never put short-term savings in the stock market — a market drop right before you need the money could derail your plan.

Does the interest rate really make a difference?

Yes, especially over longer time frames. At 4.5% APY, $400/month saved over 3 years earns roughly $900 in interest — effectively a free month of savings. At 0.01% (standard checking account), you earn almost nothing. Moving your savings to a high-yield account is one of the easiest financial wins available right now.

What if I can increase my monthly savings later?

Even small increases make a big difference. Adding $50/month to a $400 contribution shortens a 3-year goal by nearly 2 months. The best strategy is to automate your savings at your current comfortable level, then increase the amount by 10–20% each time you get a raise or pay off a debt.

Should I save for a goal while also paying off debt?

It depends on the interest rate. If your debt carries a rate higher than your savings yield (e.g., credit card at 20% vs. HYSA at 4.5%), pay off the debt first — you're mathematically losing money otherwise. However, always keep a small starter emergency fund of $500–$1,000 before aggressively paying down debt, so one setback doesn't send you back to square one.
