Frequently asked questions

What counts as an essential expense?

Essential expenses are costs you cannot avoid during a financial emergency: rent or mortgage, groceries, utilities, transportation to work, insurance premiums, and minimum debt payments. Do not include dining out, subscriptions, entertainment, or vacations — those can be cut immediately if needed.

Should I build an emergency fund before paying off debt?

Most financial experts recommend building a small starter emergency fund of $1,000 first, then aggressively paying off high-interest debt, then returning to fully fund your emergency savings. Without any cushion, any unexpected expense will send you back into debt and reset your progress.

Where should I keep my emergency fund?

Keep it in a high-yield savings account (HYSA) at a different bank from your checking account. The separation reduces the temptation to spend it, while still giving you access within 1–2 business days. Never invest your emergency fund in stocks — it could lose 30–50% right when you need it most.

Is 3 months really enough?

In 2025, job searches average 3–6 months for most professionals. Three months is a minimum baseline for people with stable employment and no dependents. If you are a freelancer, single-income household, or have children, aim for 6–9 months. The bigger your financial obligations, the larger your cushion should be.

What if I can only save a small amount each month?

Start anyway. Even $25 per week adds up to $1,300 in a year — enough to handle most common emergencies like a car repair or medical copay. Automating the transfer on payday is the most effective strategy: you never see the money, so you never miss it.
