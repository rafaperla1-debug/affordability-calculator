Frequently asked questions

What is the 28/36 rule?

The 28/36 rule is a guideline used by lenders: your housing payment should not exceed 28% of your gross monthly income, and your total debt payments (including housing) should not exceed 36%. Staying within these limits gives you the best chance of loan approval at competitive rates.

What counts as monthly debt?

Monthly debt includes credit card minimum payments, car loans, student loans, personal loans, and any other recurring debt obligations. It does not include utilities, groceries, or subscriptions. Lenders add your estimated housing payment to these existing debts to calculate your total debt-to-income ratio.

How much should I put as a down payment?

A 20% down payment is the traditional benchmark because it eliminates Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI), which adds $50–$200/month to your payment. However, many loan programs allow 3–5% down. A larger down payment lowers your monthly payment and improves your approval odds.

Does this calculator include PMI?

This calculator does not include PMI. If your down payment is less than 20% of the home price, most lenders will require PMI, which typically costs 0.5%–1.5% of the loan amount per year. Add $50–$200 to your estimated monthly payment if your down payment is below 20%.

What credit score do I need to buy a house?

Most conventional loans require a minimum credit score of 620. FHA loans can go as low as 580 with a 3.5% down payment. The higher your credit score, the better your interest rate — which directly affects how much house you can afford. A difference of just 1% in rate can change your buying power by tens of thousands of dollars.
